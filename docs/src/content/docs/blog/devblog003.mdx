## Where were we?

My last update about the engine was more than 6 months ago. The engine was more of a dialog engine with an affection stat increaser at that point.

<Image src={start} alt='Montage of various UI as features are added' />
_Left to right: Earliest UI (July 1, 2025) to more recent iterations._

The new features include:
- **Locations and Activities**. These two work together. Location objects can be **Regions** or **Locales**. A **Region** has up to 6 **Locales**. Each **Locale** has up to 4 **Activities**.
	- The macro or micro-ness of these locations is up to the game; we could have a Region that is just a single mansion, and each wing or room is a locale, or we could have a space station Region with a residential district, commercial district, downtown etc.
- **Modals**. The game now has a generic box that tells the user "this decision or action goes first". The following features make use of a modal:
	- Story-based events and dialog.
	- Inventory (display only for now).
- **Inventory**. The inventory feature currently doesn't do anything except exist and appear in the game.

## Why I built these features

The original direction was to marry incremental mechanics with otome game (dating sim) mechanics.  While developing the engine, I came to the conclusion that when mixing genres, you still need a primary genre; the secondary genre helps emphasize the mechanics of the first. In the end, I decided that **otome goes first**. That decision deprioritized two features:

1. The **passive incremental** feature, because it would intersect with dialog (while having a talk with your love interest, does the game continue? Stall?)
2.  The **autoplay feature**, because like the passive incremental feature, it intersected with too many active mechanics.

Modern incremental games expect those features.  While you can say that incremental games don't have to be idle games, there is significant overlap between the two. If I won't be building them in the near-term, then perhaps I'm not making an incremental game, after all.

### Otome goes first

I want to make female-centric romance games where there's more than dialog stat-raising based on choices. I want that the player **does something** with their love interest of choice.

What happens when one of them gets put in jail? What if you have to hide something from your not-quite-boyfriend? How will Boyfie 2 vs Boyfie 1 respond when you successfully pick a lock or beat them in pool?

### So this bag of new features belongs to what genre?

tk add image of new features

The new features are trending towards a **text-based RPG game**. Originally, the locations and activities module would have allowed for the mechanics of A Dark Room (an incremental game), but it also served to open up design for RPGs.

Otome games have stories. Stories have a who, what, when, where, why. Here's how they map to RPG features:

| Element        | RPG feature                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who            | The characters. This maps to NPCs, LIs, the MC. Includes equipment and character customization, if any. GIIN will have some support for customizing stats at the start of the game.                             |
| What           | The various things the player can do that serve to propel the game and story; this maps to Activities, Quests, Items. I haven't designed any architecture for the Quest, but it's likely to be part of the MVP. |
| When and Where | The setting of the game and story. This maps to the Locations feature.                                                                                                                                          |
| Why            | All RPG mechanics contribute to the why. Why click buttons? Because we want to imagine what choices we'd make in this or that scenario.                                                                         |

## The move away from incremental mechanics

I like A Dark Room a lot. It tells a story whilst putting you in a zen-like trance of clicking. I like the idea of Groundhog day. I also liked [Fairy Tale](https://alaynacole.com/fairy-tale). 

tk screenshot of fairy tale

The stories that lend themselves to incremental mechanics are often (not always) survivalist, or some kind of loop, or macro-event focused. They may have characters, but I haven't found one with a lot of dialog between the characters.

Ultimately, I needed a genre that was more forgiving of the dialog and choice-heavy nature of dating sims. But I also needed a model for something that wasn't too heavy, scope-wise.

### New inspirations: from loops to turns

Text-based game mechanics were pretty popular in the aughts: Mafia Wars, Kingdom of Loathing (KoL), that sort of thing. My first game engine was built with KoL in mind. These days there's Fallen London or Torn. Before all of that, I spent a few afternoons on the Palm Pilot port of Dope Wars.

tk dope wars

There were two takeaways:

- **Some randomness isn't a bad thing**. But not in most iterations of Dope Wars, where the randomness can be punishing as it does not give the user a way to beat the randomness.
- You need to provide the user with **a means to strategize their every turn**, now that you're not thinking in loops.

Expressing randomness can be very poor in older games; I found a pretty good solution in Baldur's Gate. I don't think I can do the dice animation, but I can present randomness as skill checks.

As for turn-based strategizing, I looked to the simple and elegant solution of Citizen Sleeper, in providing a random set of dice. I don't think I'll make the game that dice-dependent, but instead I have turned the mechanic into a draw of cards at the start of every turn. These cards will provide buffs (called `Drives` to the user.)
